[[ken-liu|Ken Liu]] is a Stanford CS PhD student and founder of The Open Anonymity Project. Ken's pioneering work explores the intersection between language models and data & user privacy.

## Key Takeaways

- **Unlinkable Inference**: How to separate *who you are* from *what you ask* — using cryptographic protocols so AI providers can authenticate users without learning their query content.
- **Blind Signatures**: A cryptographic primitive that enables unlinkable authentication, allowing users to obtain tokens without the token issuer learning how they are spent.
- **Secure Inference Proxies**: Routing AI queries through proxies that decouple user identity from query content, eliminating the single point of linkage between user and prompt.
- **Agent Privacy (OpenClaw)**: Why agentic AI systems amplify privacy risks — agents that browse, act, and accumulate context create far richer linkable profiles than single-turn queries.
- **Intelligence Neutrality**: A principle analogous to net neutrality — AI inference providers should route queries without inspecting or acting on their content.
- **Safety vs. Privacy Tradeoffs**: The tension between private AI inference and safety mechanisms that depend on query inspection, and why these goals may be reconcilable by design.
